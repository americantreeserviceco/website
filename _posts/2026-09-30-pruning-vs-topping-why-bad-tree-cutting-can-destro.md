Pruning removes selected branches for a specific reason, such as addressing dead or damaged wood, improving structure, or providing clearance. Topping indiscriminately cuts branches back to stubs or to limbs too small to take over as leaders. Topping can cause stress, decay, and weakly attached regrowth, and it destroys a tree's natural form.

## What purposeful pruning does

Every cut affects a tree, so pruning should have a clear objective. Depending on the tree and site, an arborist may remove dead or damaged branches, improve young-tree structure, or reduce a branch back to a suitable lateral branch. The correct approach depends on the species, condition, and reason for pruning.

## What to avoid

- Do not top a tree to make it shorter or to control its size. Ask an arborist about appropriate alternatives or whether the tree is suitable for the site.
- Avoid flush cuts that remove the branch collar and long stubs that leave dead wood. Proper cuts preserve the branch collar where present.
- Do not remove large amounts of live canopy without a site-specific plan.
- Do not climb or use a chainsaw from a ladder to prune mature trees.

Trees do not heal like people; they compartmentalize injuries. A well-placed cut can support the tree's natural response, but no cut makes a wound disappear. For more information, see the [International Society of Arboriculture's pruning guidance](https://www.treesaregood.org/Tree-Owner-Resources/Pruning-Your-Trees) and [Colorado State University Extension's mature-tree pruning guidance](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/pruning-mature-shade-trees/).

If a tree has storm damage or a branch is near a structure or utility line, keep clear and ask a qualified arborist or utility for help.
