Tree work is expensive to get wrong. A poorly pruned tree can decline for years, and a removal gone bad can damage your home, your neighbor's property or someone on the crew. Whether you're in Golden, Lakewood, Arvada, Wheat Ridge, Denver or Boulder, these seven questions will help you hire with confidence.

## 1. Are you insured, and can I see proof?

Ask for a certificate of insurance showing **general liability** and **workers' compensation** coverage. Without workers' comp, a homeowner can face questions if a worker is hurt on their property. You can call the insurer listed on the certificate to confirm the policy is active.

## 2. Is there an ISA Certified Arborist on your team?

The International Society of Arboriculture (ISA) certifies arborists who pass an exam and keep up with continuing education. You can verify a credential through the ISA's online lookup. Certification doesn't guarantee great work, but it shows a commitment to current tree-care standards.

## 3. Are you licensed to work in my city?

Some Front Range cities, including Denver, require tree service companies to be licensed, and work on public trees in the right-of-way often needs a permit. A professional company will know the rules where you live.

## 4. Will I get a written estimate?

A good estimate spells out:

- Exactly which trees and branches will be pruned or removed
- Whether stumps will be ground, and how deep
- Cleanup and haul-away of wood and debris
- Any permits required
- Price and payment terms

Compare estimates on scope, not just price. A cheaper bid often leaves out cleanup or stump work.

## 5. Do you top trees?

If the answer is yes, keep looking. Topping, cutting large branches back to stubs, is widely rejected by arborists. It causes weak, fast regrowth and decay, and it often leaves a tree more dangerous than before. Read why in [Pruning vs. Topping](/blog/pruning-vs-topping-why-bad-tree-cutting-can-destro.html).

## 6. How will you protect my property?

Ask how the crew will protect lawns, driveways, fences, gardens and roofs, and what happens if something is damaged. Professional crews use rigging to lower heavy wood instead of dropping it, and plan equipment routes ahead of time.

## 7. Can you give me references or reviews?

Look for recent reviews from homeowners near you and ask for local references. A company with a long track record in your area has a reputation to protect.

## Red flags to watch for

- Door-to-door crews showing up right after a storm
- Pressure to decide on the spot or pay in full upfront
- No written estimate, or a vague one
- No proof of insurance
- Recommending removal or topping without explaining why

## Hire before you need to

The best time to find a tree service is before a storm. Spring and summer schedules fill quickly, and late fall and winter are ideal for [pruning](/blog/dormant-season-pruning-colorado.html) and [removals](/blog/winter-tree-removal-colorado.html).
