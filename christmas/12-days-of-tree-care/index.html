  2. Tip 2

    Watering Trees in Winter When Conditions Allow

    A little winter care goes a long way. During dry stretches, trees may need supplemental water. Choose snow-free conditions with unfrozen ground, and wait until both air and soil temperatures exceed 40°F.

    Save this reminder for the next suitable warm spell.

    Related: Winter watering 101 · Full-Zone Root Nourishment

    Source: Fall and winter watering of plants and trees, Colorado State University Extension.

  3. Tip 3

    Mulching Trees for Winter Protection

    Mulch is a useful winter helper. A 2–4 inch layer of organic mulch can moderate soil conditions; keep it clear of the trunk. Think blanket, not a pile against the bark.

    Have a mulching question? Send it through our contact page.

    Related: Plant Health Care

    Source: Tips to prepare landscape trees for winter, Colorado State Forest Service.

  4. Tip 4

    Winter Care for Newly Planted Trees

    The little trees deserve a place on the nice list. Recently planted trees have smaller developing root systems and need extra attention during winter dry spells.

    Which tree did you plant most recently?

    Related: Plant Health Care · Full-Zone Root Nourishment

    Source: Tips to prepare landscape trees for winter, Colorado State Forest Service.

  5. Tip 5

    Protecting Young Tree Bark in Winter

    Winter sunshine and temperature swings can stress young, thin-barked trees. Ask about appropriate trunk protection for your tree rather than guessing at the right material or timing.

    Send us your bark-protection question through our contact page.

    Related: Preparing trees for winter storms

    Source: Tips to prepare landscape trees for winter, Colorado State Forest Service.

  6. Tip 6

    Winter Watering for Evergreen Trees

    Still green does not mean care-free. Evergreens can also benefit from winter moisture during extended dry conditions. Keep their needs in mind when checking the landscape.

    Share this reminder with an evergreen-loving neighbor.

    Related: Full-Zone Root Nourishment

    Source: Fall and winter watering of plants and trees, Colorado State University Extension.

  7. Tip 7

    Winter Dryness in Exposed Landscape Spots

    Sun, wind, and reflected heat can leave some landscape spots drier than others. Check exposed areas instead of assuming every tree has the same winter moisture needs.

    Save this tip for your next yard check.

    Related: Winter watering 101

    Source: Fall and winter watering of plants and trees, Colorado State University Extension.

    Dormant-Season Pruning: Plan Before You Cut

    Put thoughtful pruning on the calendar. Dormant-season pruning suits many trees, but the right timing and cuts depend on the tree and its condition. Make a plan before reaching for the tools.
