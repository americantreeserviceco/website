Lit-up trees are one of the best parts of December in Front Range neighborhoods. But every winter, arborists see the after-effects: wires that cut into bark, branches broken by ladders, and lights left on so long the tree grows around them. A few simple habits will keep your trees healthy and your family safe.

## Check the tree before you climb

Before anyone gets on a ladder, look up:

- **Dead or broken limbs** above where you'll stand or lean a ladder
- **Hangers,** broken branches caught in the canopy after fall storms
- **Cracks** in large limbs or where trunks divide
- **Branches near power lines.** Never hang lights in a tree that touches or grows near overhead lines.

If you spot any of these, have them dealt with before decorating. A limb that looks fine from the ground can fail under a person's weight or a ladder's pressure. See our [homeowner's tree safety checklist](/blog/how-to-tell-if-your-tree-is-dangerous-homeowners-safety-checklist.html).

## Decorating tips that protect the tree

1. **Wrap loosely.** Lights should rest on branches, not cinch around them. Leave room so wind and snow can move the branches.
2. **Skip nails, staples and screws.** Every hole in the bark is an entry point for insects and decay. Use soft ties or plastic clips that don't pierce bark.
3. **Use LED lights.** They run cooler, use less power and are lighter on small branches.
4. **Keep weight off small limbs.** Heavy strands, ornaments and inflatables can break thin branches, especially once snow adds weight.
5. **Use outdoor-rated cords and GFCI outlets.** Keep connections off the ground and away from melting snow.
6. **Never lean a ladder on a weak branch.** Use a stable, level ladder and have someone hold it. For tall trees, consider hiring help.

## Take lights down by spring

Lights wrapped around a trunk or branch don't hurt much in one winter, but left on for years, they become a noose. As the tree grows, wires cut into the bark and can girdle the branch, cutting off water and nutrients. Plan to remove wraps before growth starts in spring.

## Snow and lights

A Front Range snowstorm can add a lot of weight to branches already carrying lights. If heavy, wet snow is in the forecast, gently brush snow off small trees and shrubs from the ground with a broom, pushing up and off.
