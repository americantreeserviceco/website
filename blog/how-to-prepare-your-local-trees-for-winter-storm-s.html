How to Prepare Your Trees for Winter Storm Season

Heavy, wet snow, ice, and wind can damage trees, especially when they expose existing defects or broken branches. Homeowners can watch for changes and plan routine care, but pruning large trees and storm-damaged limbs is hazardous work for trained professionals.

Inspect from the ground

Before winter weather arrives, look from a safe distance for broken or dead branches, fresh cracks, a sudden lean, lifted soil, or limbs hanging over places people use. These signs do not by themselves determine whether a tree needs removal. If you see a significant defect, ask a qualified arborist to assess it.

Arrange pruning when needed

Pruning should have a clear purpose, such as removing dead or damaged wood or addressing a structural concern. A qualified arborist makes cuts at appropriate branch unions; routine branch removal is not done by cutting every limb at a 45-degree angle or leaving arbitrary stubs. Do not climb a ladder, use a chainsaw overhead, or prune near power lines.

Support tree health

  • Water during prolonged dry periods when the soil is not frozen, including in fall and winter when conditions allow.
  • Apply mulch over the root area where practical, keeping it away from the trunk.
  • Avoid piling snow, soil, or equipment against the trunk and roots.
  • Do not wrap trees in products advertised as ice protection or apply sprays unless a qualified professional has identified a specific need.

During and after a storm

Keep away from trees with loaded, broken, or hanging limbs. Do not shake branches, strike them, or try to remove ice. If a tree or branch contacts a power line, stay well away and call the utility.

After the storm, look from a safe location for new cracks, hanging limbs, a sudden lean, or lifted soil. Keep people away from the affected area and contact a qualified tree professional for significant damage.

Preparing trees does not make them stormproof. Regular observation and appropriate professional care can help identify concerns before the next storm.
