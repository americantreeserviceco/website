Ash trees are everywhere along the Front Range. They were planted widely in neighborhoods, parks and along streets because they grow quickly and tolerate Colorado conditions. That popularity is now a problem. Emerald ash borer (EAB), an invasive beetle that kills ash trees, was first found in Colorado in Boulder in 2013 and has since been confirmed in a growing number of Front Range communities.

If you have an ash tree in Golden, Lakewood, Arvada, Wheat Ridge, Denver or Boulder, now is the time to decide what to do about it.

## Is your tree an ash?

- **Leaves:** compound, with 5 to 11 leaflets per leaf.
- **Branching:** branches and buds grow directly opposite each other.
- **Bark:** on mature trees, a tight diamond-shaped pattern of ridges.

Mountain ash is *not* a true ash and isn't affected by EAB. If you're unsure, an arborist can identify your tree in a few minutes.

## Warning signs of emerald ash borer

- **Thinning canopy,** usually starting at the top of the tree
- **D-shaped exit holes** about 1/8 inch across in the bark
- **S-shaped tunnels** under loose or split bark
- **Woodpecker damage,** where birds strip outer bark to get at larvae and leave light-colored patches
- **Sprouts** from the trunk or base as the tree struggles

By the time symptoms are obvious, a tree may have been infested for a few years. That's why a professional assessment matters.

## Your three options

### 1. Protect a healthy, valuable ash

Healthy ash trees can be protected with systemic insecticide treatments, typically trunk injections applied by a licensed pesticide applicator and repeated on a schedule. Treatment is usually worth considering for trees that are in good condition, well placed and important to your property's shade and value.

### 2. Remove a declining ash

Trees that have already lost a large share of their canopy are generally poor candidates for treatment. Dead or declining trees can become increasingly hazardous as they deteriorate, so have them assessed and plan any needed [tree removal](/services/tree-removal/) with a qualified professional.

### 3. Plan a replacement

Many homeowners remove a struggling ash and plant a different species, so shade returns sooner. Choosing a diverse mix of trees protects your yard from the next pest that targets a single species.

## Why winter is a good time to decide

- Dormant ash trees can be assessed for structure and past canopy decline.
- Winter is a good season for [removals](/blog/winter-tree-removal-colorado.html), with firm ground and dormant landscapes.
- Deciding now gives you time to plan spring treatment for trees worth saving.

## Help slow the spread

Avoid moving ash firewood or wood debris, and check current state and local rules before transporting it. Moving infested wood can help spread EAB to new areas.
